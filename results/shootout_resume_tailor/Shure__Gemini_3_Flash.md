### 1. RESUME TAILORING

Shure isn't looking for a "mixer"; they are looking for a **Subject Matter Expert (SME)** who can talk to IT Directors and AV Integrators. We need to pivot your resume from "Show Operator" to "Systems Consultant."

**1. Professional Profile Reframing**
*   **BEFORE:** "A1 audio engineer with 8+ years of experience spanning large-scale festival/concert production and high-end corporate AV..."
*   **AFTER:** "Technical Solutions Specialist & RF Coordinator with 8+ years of experience designing and deploying complex audio ecosystems for Fortune 500 enterprise clients and national broadcast events. Expert in Shure Axient Digital and Wireless Workbench, managing 130+ channel environments. Leverages a background in Accounting to align technical AV/IT architecture with corporate ROI and project KPIs."
*   **WHY:** This uses Shure’s internal language ("Solutions," "Ecosystems") and immediately highlights your accounting degree as a business asset, not a career pivot.

**2. Elevating RF Coordination to "Design & Consulting"**
*   **BEFORE:** "Served as RF engineer for the 2025 WNBA All-Star Game... providing full arena RF coordination."
*   **AFTER:** "Engineered and managed mission-critical RF spectrum architecture for the 2025 WNBA All-Star Game (128 channels). Conducted site surveys, frequency coordination, and real-time interference mitigation to ensure 100% broadcast reliability."
*   **WHY:** "Architecture" and "Site Surveys" are pre-sales activities. Shure needs to know you can walk into a stadium or HQ and "own" the spectrum design.

**3. Highlighting the IT/Network Layer**
*   **BEFORE:** "Dante, AES67, MADI" (Listed under Networking/Protocols)
*   **AFTER:** "Advanced AoIP Networking: Expert in Dante and AES67 implementation across converged corporate networks. Proficient in managing IP addressing, VLAN tagging for audio traffic, and troubleshooting network latency in multi-room enterprise deployments."
*   **WHY:** This role is "AV/IT." You must prove you speak "IT" (VLANs, Latency, Converged Networks) so the IT Director trusts your recommendations.

**4. Transitioning "Freelance" to "Consultative Relationship Management"**
*   **BEFORE:** "Maintain trusted repeat relationships with 7–8 production companies... a track record of professional reliability."
*   **AFTER:** "Act as a primary Technical Consultant for 8+ key regional production partners. Responsibilities include qualifying project requirements, specifying equipment manifests, and delivering customized technical solutions to meet end-user business objectives."
*   **WHY:** The job description asks for "Qualifying projects" and "Customer Needs Analysis." This framing proves you’ve been doing "pre-sales" for your own freelance business for years.

**5. Framing the Ramova Theater Install as "Integration"**
*   **BEFORE:** "Contributed to the install team for the first L-ISA immersive audio installation... (primarily A Series cabinets)"
*   **AFTER:** "Solutions Engineering & Integration: Collaborated on the commissioning of the Midwest’s first L-ISA immersive audio system. Coordinated with electrical and IT contractors to ensure hardware integration met architectural and network specifications."
*   **WHY:** Shure works heavily with Integrators. You need to show you understand the "Install" side of the house, not just the "Live" side.

**6. Adding the "Business/CRM" Element (ADD to Professional Experience)**
*   **ADD:** "Business Operations & Pipeline Management: Utilize disciplined record-keeping and client management (aligned with CRM best practices) to maintain a 90% client retention rate. Manage project budgets and technical riders to ensure fiscal alignment with client expectations."
*   **PLACEMENT:** Under "Freelance Audio Engineer" experience.
*   **WHY:** Directly addresses the "Salesforce/KPI/Sales Targets" requirement by showing you have a "sales-minded" approach to your freelance career.

### 3. INTERVIEW PREP

**Likely Technical Questions:**
1.  **"Walk us through your coordination workflow for a 130-channel event in a crowded RF environment like a hotel or stadium."** (Focus on Wireless Workbench, Coordination Zones, and IMD Management.)
2.  **"How do you handle a situation where a client’s IT department is hesitant to put Dante traffic on their primary corporate network?"** (Talk about VLANs, QoS, and bandwidth requirements.)
3.  **"What is the biggest difference in designing for a live concert vs. a permanent corporate conferencing install?"** (Focus on "Ease of use" for the end-user vs. "Total control" for the engineer.)
4.  **"If you're at a site visit and the MXA920 (ceiling array) isn't picking up a presenter in the back of the room, what are the first three things you check?"** (Focus on DSP lobes, gain structure, and AEC.)
5.  **"How do you explain the value of Axient Digital over a lower-cost ULX-D system to a budget-conscious client?"** (Focus on spectral efficiency, High Density mode, and interference detection/avoidance.)

**Behavioral/Transition Questions:**
1.  **"You’ve been a freelancer for a long time. Why shift to a corporate role at Shure now?"** 
    *   *Answer:* "I’ve reached a point where I want to influence the *design* and *strategy* of the technology, not just operate it. Shure is the standard I’ve relied on for years, and I want to help other organizations implement that same reliability at scale."
2.  **"This role requires 50% travel and a lot of 'prep' work. How do you stay organized?"** (Mention your accounting background and how you manage your current 70+ events/year schedule.)
3.  **"Describe a time you had to explain a complex technical issue to a non-technical stakeholder (like a CEO)."** (Use the story of the AbbVie or Institutional Investor events.)

**Stories to Prepare (The "Hero" Stories):**
*   **The "Mid-Show Rescue":** Tell the story of taking over the RF coordination mid-show when the A2 failed. This proves "Expert Troubleshooting" and "Leadership."
*   **The "UAD Luna Pivot":** Use this to show "Adaptability" and "Signal Flow Mastery."
*   **The "Glades Festival Build":** Use this to show "System Design" and "Documentation"—building something from nothing is exactly what "Solution Engineering" is.

**Questions to ASK the Interviewer:**
1.  **"How does the Solutions Engineering team collaborate with the Product Development team to relay field feedback on new gear?"** (Shows you care about the long-term roadmap.)
2.  **"In the NYC market, what is the biggest 'IT' hurdle our customers are currently facing when integrating Shure systems?"** (Shows you're thinking about the specific local challenges.)
3.  **"What does a successful first 90 days look like for this role? Is the focus more on technical training or client relationship building?"** (Shows you are goal-oriented.)
