### 3. INTERVIEW PREP

#### LIKELY TECHNICAL QUESTIONS

**1. "Walk us through how you'd plan and coordinate a 130-channel Axient Digital deployment in a hotel environment with 30+ simultaneous rooms."**
- This is your AbbVie story. They're testing whether you actually did the work or just assisted. Go deep: talk about frequency scanning, exclusion zones, intermod calculations in Wireless Workbench, how you handled bleed between adjacent ballroom partitions, backup frequency plans, and real-time monitoring during the show. Mention specific Axient Digital features — Spectrum Manager, ShowLink, channel quality meters.

**2. "A customer is deciding between Shure Axient Digital and Sennheiser Digital 6000 for a new corporate boardroom install. How would you position Axient?"**
- They want to see if you can sell, not just operate. Talk about Spectrum Manager's real-time frequency management, ShowLink control, encryption, the Wireless Workbench ecosystem, and Dante integration — then tie it to business value: reduced setup time, fewer failures, lower total cost of ownership. Be honest about where competitors have strengths too — Shure respects intellectual honesty.

**3. "Explain how Dante works at a networking level. What happens when you plug a Shure device into a Dante network?"**
- You're Dante Level 2 certified, so they'll expect you to go beyond the basics. Discuss multicast vs. unicast, clock synchronization (PTP), latency settings, subnet considerations, and how Dante Controller manages routing. Connect it to real scenarios — "On the AbbVie deployment, I had Axient Digital receivers feeding Dante into the console, and here's how I managed the network topology..."

**4. "A customer asks you to design a wireless microphone solution for a 15-room corporate training facility. Walk us through your approach."**
- This is a solution design question. Start with needs analysis (how many simultaneous presenters per room, content type, recording requirements), then RF site survey, then product recommendation (likely Axient Digital or potentially MXW/Microflex for installed rooms), frequency coordination plan, network architecture, and ongoing monitoring. They want to see the consultative process, not just the gear list.

**5. "What's your experience with Shure's installed/conferencing products — MXA920, P300, IntelliMix?"**
- Be honest: your experience is primarily with Axient Digital in live/corporate event contexts. But then pivot: "I understand DSP routing, AEC, and networked audio architecture from my Dante certification and field experience. The conferencing product line is a natural extension of skills I already have — and frankly, the RF coordination complexity I manage with Axient is significantly more challenging than a ceiling array deployment." Don't fake expertise you don't have. They'll smell it instantly.

**6. "How would you use Wireless Workbench to troubleshoot intermittent dropouts on a client's wireless system?"**
- Walk through it step by step: check the frequency coordination for intermod conflicts, review the RF scan for new interference sources, check ShowLink data for antenna coverage gaps, review channel quality history, examine gain structure (if transmitter input is too hot or too low), and check for physical issues (antenna placement, cable runs). Mention that you'd document findings and recommend solutions — that's the SE part.

**7. "Tell me about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical stakeholder."**
- You mix for Fortune 500 executives and governors. Pick a specific moment — maybe explaining to an event planner at Institutional Investor why you needed more Axient channels than they budgeted for, or why the RF environment in a specific hotel required a different approach than the last venue. Frame it as: I translated technical requirements into business language and got buy-in.
