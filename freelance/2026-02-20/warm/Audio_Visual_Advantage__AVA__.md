## Company Assessment

Audio Visual Advantage is a privately owned, full-service AV production company headquartered in Las Vegas with branches in St. Louis and Washington, D.C. They serve the corporate meetings and conventions market — general sessions, breakout rooms, pipe and drape — and position themselves as an independent alternative to expensive hotel in-house AV vendors. Their long-standing relationship with the American Soybean Association (15+ years) indicates they have a real, recurring client base and operational history. That said, nothing in their public-facing materials indicates large-format audio production, concert-level PA systems, or RF coordination work at the scale Eric handles.

The website's language is notably presentation- and display-centric — heavy on "PowerPoint presentations," "staging," and "video production" — with audio treated as a supporting element rather than a primary technical focus. There's no gear list, no mention of L-Acoustics, DiGiCo, Shure Axient, or Dante, and the emphasis on "breakout rooms and smaller meetings" suggests their typical audio footprint is confidence monitors, lapel mics, and basic mixing rather than complex A1 work. The "20+ years of experience" claim for their AV coordinator is a positive signal for professionalism, but it also implies a senior, possibly owner-operator structure that may not regularly need freelance A1s.

Most critically for Eric, there is no Chicago or Midwest presence. Las Vegas is a full domestic flight away, and while D.C. and St. Louis are closer geographically, neither is in his established market. Unless AVA is actively crewing national traveling shows that happen to route through the Midwest, the logistical case for this relationship is weak.

## Why They Would Want the Candidate

- Eric's deep corporate AV background (pharmaceutical meetings, multi-room hotel deployments, breakout coordination) maps directly onto AVA's core service offering
- His RF coordination experience managing 130 channels across 30+ simultaneous breakout rooms is exactly the skill set AVA's complex hotel-property shows would demand
- His track record of reliability and stepping into elevated roles mid-show would appeal to a small owner-operated company that needs people they can trust without handholding

## Potential Red Flags

- No Chicago/Midwest office means day-call work is essentially impossible without travel cost negotiation
- No visible gear list makes it impossible to confirm technical alignment — may be running prosumer or entry-level audio systems
- Small owner-operated structure ("Jim" appears to be the principal) likely means limited freelance call volume and possibly a tight, already-established crew with few open slots
