## Company Assessment

Masque Sound is one of the most storied names in American theatrical sound — founded in 1936, they've been the house sound provider for landmark Broadway productions including The Book of Mormon, Harry Potter and the Cursed Child, and Kimberly Akimbo, as well as permanent installation work like the Baltimore Center Stage renovation. They operate out of East Rutherford, NJ, just outside New York City, and their reputation in the theatrical world is unimpeachable. This is unambiguously a real, operating, high-caliber company.

The problem is that virtually nothing about Masque's business model aligns with Eric's freelance profile. Theatrical sound is a highly specialized discipline with its own console ecosystem (largely Avid VENUE/S6L, DiGiCo, and theatrical-specific signal chains), its own crew culture, and its own hiring patterns — typically drawing from a deep bench of NYC-based engineers with years of theatrical experience. Eric's strengths are in corporate general sessions, festivals, RF coordination for live broadcast events, and L-Acoustics deployment — none of which map onto what Masque does day to day.

Additionally, the geographic gap is significant. East Rutherford is ~800 miles from Chicago, making this impractical for day calls. Even if Masque occasionally needed a touring or road engineer, the overlap in work type is thin enough that this relationship is unlikely to develop organically or produce recurring work.
