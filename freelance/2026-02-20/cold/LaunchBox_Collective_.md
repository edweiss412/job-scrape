## Company Assessment

The facility spans 5,600 sq ft and is positioned as a turnkey brand experience and broadcast environment — think keynotes, brand launches, leadership summits, and filmed content rather than large-scale concerts or major corporate general sessions. Their production language skews heavily toward broadcast, camera systems, and brand storytelling rather than large-format live audio. The mention of Avid, SSL, and RF in the gear signals is promising on paper, but in context it almost certainly refers to a modest studio/broadcast setup rather than a touring-grade live audio rig.

The core business model here is venue rental with in-house production bundled in. That structure is a yellow flag for freelance A1 outreach — when a venue owns its production team and bills it as a service differentiator ("let us handle the setup, you show up and shine"), there's limited structural need to bring in outside engineers. Any freelance audio work would likely be overflow or specialty coverage, not a recurring day-call relationship. The scale — a single 5,600 sq ft room in Fort Worth — doesn't suggest the event volume or complexity that would justify pulling in a Chicago-based A1 at travel day rates.

Fort Worth is also a meaningful logistical obstacle. It's a domestic flight from Chicago (roughly 2.5 hours), and with no established relationship or regional production network connection, the economics of travel day rates plus airfare make day-call viability low unless Eric already has other Texas work to stack around it. The company appears real and well-organized, but it simply isn't the right profile — too small, too far, and too self-contained.
